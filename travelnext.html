Case study · Globant

TravelNext

Designing a vision for the future Globant travel management solution.

  • PlatformWeb & mobile
  • Part ofGlow portal
  • Scope80+ screens

01 / Overview

Overview

TravelNext is a digital platform to make official travel easier for Globers. It is a part of the Glow portal and has a web and mobile view.

This platform helps Globers to travel across Globant offices & client locations by booking flights, buses, cabs, hotels & other services. It is used by Globers, GT, PM & various other roles.

The project was an extension of TravelApp-1, which was a travel portal for Globers.

02 / Problem

Problem statement & goals

Legacy screens (existing)

Major pain points

  • Big friction while naming the trip
  • Cost error
  • Redundancy in add passenger
  • Complexity of billability

Problem statement

  • Enhance user experience & usability for the existing Travel web app.
  • Migrate it as a supplementary product of Glow on the Glow platform.
  • Design this app as a product Globant can publish in the future as a white-label product.
  • A mobile application does not exist to access the Travel app on mobile platforms.
  • The web UI is not user friendly.
  • You have to depend on Globant travel agents (GT) to create a trip.
  • The GT has to fill in a lot of data manually while creating the trip for any Glober.
  • A Glober cannot see his trip itinerary while traveling.

Goals

  • Improve user experience
  • Give the right solution by testing the utility
  • Make a simpler create-trip flow for Globers by excluding a few steps from the existing design
  • Reduce the rejection rate of trips internally
  • Allow communication with all user roles associated with a trip
  • Create a mobile app so a Glober can see his trip info while traveling
  • Design as a product so Globant can sell it later

Persona 1

Kaylee Ryan

Travel Agent · 30, Female · Mexico

Kaylee is the travel agent in the organisation. Her job is to verify and approve all the requested trips by Globers. She receives almost 10+ business trip requests every day and has to respond to them before the trip expires. Due to the limitations of the current platform, she has to fill all the data manually for each passenger, and it's very time-consuming.

Frustrations

  • Too much time spent approving trip bookings - she is busy!
  • Always needs to be in touch with Globers to get all the information in place

Needs / goals

  • Minimal steps to approve trips, and save time
  • Get all the information from the Glober through the application
  • Book the reservation through customized budget plans
  • Glober preferences for flight, hotel and services should be filled in by them in the app

Workflow improvements

  • Better user interface for the web application
  • Enhance user experience
  • Mobile application to track itinerary and to create a trip from mobile

Persona 2

John Lawson

Marketing · 37, Male · India

John is a marketing person at Globant who travels 2-3 times each month for his job. He often visits the same city for client visits for new business. He is frustrated that no matter how frequently he takes similar trips, he spends hours of his day booking travel. He expects his travel solutions to be as organized as he is.

Frustrations

  • Too much time spent booking - he is a regular traveler!
  • Too many websites visited per trip to compare flight and hotel bookings
  • Too much time spent finding the best deals

Needs / goals

  • Quickly get the information he needs when traveling
  • Maximise his loyalty points and rewards
  • Narrow his options when it comes to finding a deal
  • Would like to create/book a trip on his phone

Workflow improvements

  • Better control over global elements
  • Spend less time creating a trip

Persona 3

Alice Gonzalez

Software Developer · 37, Female · New York

Alice works at an IT firm in New York as a Lead Software Engineer. She is working at a client location at the US office. Due to the complex booking system she has to take help from her manager and the travel agent whenever she needs to create a trip.

Frustrations

  • Always takes help from a manager or travel agent (GT)
  • Too much time to find information to plan the trip

Needs / goals

  • Prefers a single airline for domestic travel
  • A user interface that flows freely

Workflow improvements

  • Better control over global elements
  • Spend less time creating a trip
  • Easy flow for trip creation

What was the goal?

  • Understand the user's task as a Travel Agent / PM / Glober, identify how they react to the design of the Travel application and how easily they can perform with the proposed design.
  • Understand users' needs and gather feedback on the product and their expectations from it.

Methodology

  • Globers were asked to see and navigate both web designs. Using the "thinking aloud" method, we collected their comments in an interview guide.
  • Participants answered a general questionnaire about their experience creating a trip as a Glober or GT for 15-20 minutes, then were given 2 design options to perform tasks on the Travel application.
Part 01

Personal interview

To understand the user's needs as a Project Manager or Glober traveler, a set of questions was asked.

Participants: 10   Duration: 15-30 minutes

Part 02

Usability test of wireframes

To validate the design, users were asked to create a trip using 4 steps: Create > Cost > Passenger > Itinerary.

Participants: 10   Duration: 30-45 minutes

Who participated?

Because of the nature of the tool, random Globers were interviewed: users who had never used Travel before, some who had, and others who use it frequently. Ten participants took part, including GT, PM, BA, developers, facility managers and other Globers.

Usability results

  • Users didn't have much problem with the designed screens; both design options were clear on the action the user was going to take. Approach 2's look and feel was liked more, but they wanted the stepper of Approach 1.
  • Users feel more comfortable with the bigger cards but are still confused about everything going on in the screen. They need more connection between the total percentage and the projects added.
  • Users had the same issues with the cost allocation with the big cards, having too many actions present (me, Glober, non-Glober, save, add new), but liked the look and feel of the second approach more.
  • There is a lack of presence of business rules that could confuse users about the sequence. They were still more comfortable with the look and feel of the second approach.
  • Participants also struggled with some actions, for example add cost allocation and project allocation.
